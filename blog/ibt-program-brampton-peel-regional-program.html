IBT Program in Brampton: A Parent's Guide to Peel's Regional Program

  • By Mr. Grewal
  • November 15, 2026
  • 6 Min

Which Schools Offer It, When to Apply, and How Your Child Can Get Ready

If your child is in Grade 5 or Grade 8 in Brampton, you may have heard other parents mention "IBT" — and wondered whether it is something your child should apply for. IBT stands for International Business and Technology, one of the Peel District School Board's Regional Learning Choices Programs. It is not a private school, and it does not cost tuition. It is a specialized stream inside a regular public school, built for students who are curious about business and technology. Here is what Brampton parents need to know — including that applications for September 2027 are open right now, and they close on November 24.

1. What the IBT program actually is

IBT students follow the regular Ontario curriculum, but with a business and technology focus woven through it. In Grades 9 and 10, they take business and/or technology courses alongside their core subjects, and French in both years. They are grouped together in IBT classes, so they move through the program as a cohort with classmates who share the same interests. Students who like building things, solving problems, and understanding how businesses work get to spend more of their school day doing exactly that — while still earning the same Ontario Secondary School Diploma as everyone else.

2. The two IBT streams Brampton parents should know

There are two separate IBT programs in Peel, with different entry points. The elementary program runs at Williams Parkway Senior Public School, right here in Brampton — the only entry point is Grade 6, which means students apply while they are in Grade 5. The secondary program runs at two high schools: North Park Secondary School in Brampton and Meadowvale Secondary School in Mississauga. Students apply in Grade 8 to start in Grade 9. One important detail: being in the elementary program does not guarantee a spot in the secondary program. Every student who wants the high school stream has to apply again, through the same process as everyone else.

3. Key dates for September 2027 entry

For the secondary program, the Peel board has set the application window for the 2027-28 school year from November 3, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. to November 24, 2026 at 11:00 p.m. Applications are submitted online through the board's regional programs website. First-round offers go out on February 2, 2027 after 3 p.m., and waitlist offers run from March 30 to September 9, 2027. If your child is in Grade 8 right now, this is a short window — about three weeks — so mark November 24 on the calendar today. The elementary program at Williams Parkway runs on its own timeline, so check the board's elementary regional programs page for this year's dates rather than assuming they match the high school ones.

4. How students are chosen

There is no entrance exam for secondary IBT. Instead, the board looks at two things: your child's Grade 7 report card and a short video submission of no more than 90 seconds. That means the marks your child earned last year matter more than anything they do this fall — the Grade 7 report card is already set. The video is your child's chance to show who they are: what interests them about business or technology, and why they want to be in the program. Keep it simple and genuine. A student talking honestly about a lemonade stand, a coding project, or a small business idea will stand out far more than a stiff, scripted speech.

5. Is IBT the right fit for your child?

IBT suits students who are organized, curious, and genuinely interested in business or technology — not just students with the highest marks. Because IBT students are cohorted together, the pace can be brisk and the expectations high. Ask your child: Do they enjoy projects where they build or create something? Do they like figuring out how things work, or how businesses work? If the answer is mostly yes, IBT is worth applying for. If your child is more drawn to arts, science, or skilled trades, Peel offers regional programs in those areas too — the right program is the one that matches your child's actual interests.

6. How Grade 5 students can prepare for elementary IBT

If your child is in Grade 5 and hoping for the Williams Parkway program next year, this is the year to build the habits that make a strong application. Keep up steady work in math and language — those are the subjects that carry the most weight. Give your child real chances to show initiative: let them plan a small project, run a bake sale, help budget for a family outing, or try a simple coding activity. Talk to their current teacher about the program and ask for an honest read on whether your child would thrive in a faster-paced, project-heavy classroom. And go to the information night when the school announces it — you will hear directly from the teachers who run the program.

7. How tutoring can help

Because secondary IBT admission leans heavily on the Grade 7 report card, there is not much a Grade 8 student can do to change that part of the picture — but there is still plenty worth doing. If your child's math or language marks dipped in Grade 7, targeted help now can get them back on track before high school starts, so they walk into Grade 9 ready for the faster pace of a cohorted program. An Ontario-certified teacher can also help your child prepare the video submission: planning what to say, practising until it sounds natural, and keeping it inside 90 seconds. And for Grade 5 students aiming at Williams Parkway, a few focused sessions on math confidence and organized writing now can make a real difference to the report card that matters most.

The IBT program is a genuine opportunity for Brampton students who love business and technology — but the window to apply is short, and it only opens once a year. If your child is in Grade 8, the deadline is November 24. If your child is in Grade 5, start the conversation now. Either way, the best preparation is the same: strong habits, real curiosity, and a child who can explain in their own words why they want to be there.
