How Much Does Tutoring Cost in Brampton & Ontario? (2026 Parent Guide)

  • By Mr. Grewal
  • September 23, 2026
  • 6 Min

The Honest Price Breakdown, From Someone Who Charges for It

If you have ever typed "how much does tutoring cost Ontario" into Google, you know what happens next: a mess of vague answers. Some sites say $25 an hour, others say $100, and nobody tells you why the gap is so big. As a parent, you end up stuck between two worries — overpaying for something you do not need, or paying too little and getting help that does not actually help. So let me lay it out plainly, the way I would explain it to a parent sitting across from me in Brampton.

1. The honest 2026 price table

Here are the ranges you will actually see across Brampton and Ontario right now. Student and peer tutors — university or college students tutoring on the side — typically charge around $25 an hour. General tutoring centres with hired staff usually land somewhere between $30 and $50 an hour, depending on the subject and format. Tutoring from an Ontario-certified teacher — someone licensed by the Ontario College of Teachers who teaches the curriculum for a living — generally runs $40 to $60+ an hour. And specialists — for things like IEP support, advanced sciences, or university-level prep — can run $60 to $100+ an hour. That is the full spread. Any provider quoting far outside these bands should be able to explain exactly why.

2. What ~$25/hour student tutoring actually gets you

There is nothing wrong with student tutors — some are excellent, especially for homework help and basic practice with younger kids. But know what you are buying: help with tonight's assignment, from someone who is learning the material themselves not long ago. A student tutor generally does not have formal training in the Ontario curriculum, cannot diagnose why your child is really struggling, and will not be structuring a term-by-term learning plan. If your child just needs a friendly homework buddy, $25 an hour can be fine. If your child is falling behind grade level or has real gaps, it usually is not.

3. What the certified-teacher premium buys you

The jump from $25 to $40–$60 an hour is the jump from homework help to actual teaching, and here is what sits inside that difference. A certified Ontario teacher knows the curriculum expectations for each grade — not just how to solve a problem, but which curriculum standard the problem maps to and what your child's teacher will test next. They can diagnose the real gap: a Grade 7 student struggling with fractions often has a Grade 5 place-value hole underneath it, and finding that hole takes a trained eye. They assess properly, track progress against report-card strands, and give you straight answers at parent-teacher-interview level, not guesses. You are paying for professional accountability — the same person who stands in a classroom all day, standing behind your child's progress.

4. Small groups vs. one-on-one: where the math favours you

One-on-one tutoring is the fastest way to close a gap, but it is also the most expensive option per hour. Small-group tutoring — typically up to four students with one teacher — can cut the hourly cost nearly in half while keeping a certified teacher leading the session. Groups work best when the kids are close in grade and level, so the teaching stays targeted. If the group is too big or too mixed, it becomes crowd control with a worksheet. Always ask how many students are actually in the room, and how the provider keeps them grouped by ability.

5. Red flags of too-cheap tutoring

If a deal looks too good, slow down and look closer. Common red flags: no real information about who will actually teach your child; a flat rock-bottom rate with no mention of qualifications; worksheets instead of teaching, where the tutor mostly sits while your child works alone; no plan, no progress updates, and no sense of where your child is headed; and pressure to pay for a big package before your child has even had a single session. Cheap help that does not move the needle is not cheap — it is money and months you do not get back.

6. Questions to ask before you pay anything

Whatever you are quoted, ask these before committing: Is the tutor an Ontario-certified teacher? What is actually included in the hourly rate — materials, assessments, parent updates? How many students are in each session? How will you measure my child's progress, and how will I see it? Is there a contract or a lock-in, or can we stop anytime? And can my child try a session before we commit? A confident provider answers every one of these without dodging. If the answers are vague, walk away — no matter how good the price sounds.

The real question was never "what is the cheapest option" — it is "what does my child actually need, and what will it cost to get it."
